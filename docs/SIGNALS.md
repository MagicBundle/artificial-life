# Information in the signals of `runs/64k_s0`

Do the chemical emissions and messages that agents still produce carry information about their situation?
Mutual information (MI) between each signal channel and the agent's resource, harvest, energy, neighbourhood
and next-step behaviour, at eight states of the 64k run (seed 0, 1.504M steps), against shuffle nulls. Computed
offline with `src/alife/analysis/` (`mi`, `resim`, `signals`). Written 2026-10-12. Analysis only: the engine,
the step, the config and the golden digest are unchanged.

## Answer

**Yes. The signals that remain carry more information about the sender's situation than the more frequent
signals of the early run did.** Emitter frequency fell from 82 % at 120k to 26 % at the end, while the
information in `emit0`, `emit1`, `msg0` and `msg1` rose from almost nothing to 0.1–0.38 bits. As Mitri,
Floreano & Keller (2010) warn, frequency is a poor guide to information. "Signalling was abandoned" in
`HANDOVER.md` is true of how many agents emit, not of what the remaining signals say.

- **What they carry:** mostly *local crowding* and the agent's *next movement*, and these two are largely one
  state. Over the run, crowding and next-step thrust become coupled (0.02 bits at 120k, 0.28 at the end), and
  crowding tracks the resource of the cell (0.29 → 0.44 bits): agents cluster on rich cells and stop. Smaller,
  robust information about resource: `msg0` 0.11 bits, `emit1` 0.07 (0.10 among its emitters), `msg1` 0.05.
  Energy is weak: at most 0.05 bits, in `msg0` and `msg1` late in the run.
- **Kin:** the apparent kin information is crowding (98 % of sensed neighbours are same-clade at the end).
  Given the number of neighbours, kin composition adds at most 0.05 bits, and robustly only once.
- **Intent:** next-step thrust is predicted robustly from 250k on (up to 0.32 bits from `msg0`), turn weakly
  (≤ 0.07), attack never (≤ 0.03 bits, never significant).
- **`emit2`**, the slow wide-diffusing channel, carries nothing robust except at 1M, where crowding and
  intent information (0.12 and 0.10 bits) lies almost entirely in *whether* an agent emits (0.02 among emitters);
  it is gone again by 1.49M.
- **Change over the run:**
  - Step 0, random founder brains: no significant information anywhere.
  - 120k (88 clades): little population-wide information, about twice as much within patches (`msg1`–crowding
    0.06 bits overall, 0.13 within patches), and as much or more within clades (0.08). Lineages and regions use
    their own codes.
  - From 250k (22 clades): robust crowding and intent information appears in four channels and stays.
  - `msg0` keeps strengthening to the end (crowding 0.11 → 0.34 bits, intent 0.09 → 0.29, resource up to 0.11
    after 1M), as one clade takes over (84 %).
- **Within lineages:** conditioning on the agent's own clade keeps about 0.1 bits of crowding information in
  `msg0`, `msg1` and `emit1` at the end. The signals track the individual's situation within a lineage, not
  only heritable differences between lineages. The strong `msg0`→thrust link is the exception: 0.29 bits
  overall but 0.02 within clades at the end, so it is mostly a difference between clades that both message
  and move differently.

What this does not show: whether receivers use the information, or whether the signals are honest or deceptive
in Mitri et al.'s sense. That needs the receivers' side (§6).

## 2. Details

**Coupling of the targets** (excess MI, bits, same estimator, 6,000 agents, 20 shuffles):

| pair | 120k | 250k | 1.504M |
|---|---|---|---|
| crowd – thrust_next | 0.02 | 0.14 | 0.28 |
| crowd – resource | 0.29 | 0.34 | 0.44 |
| crowd – harvest | 0.12 | 0.12 | 0.18 |
| crowd – energy | 0.00 | 0.02 | 0.02 |
| kin – crowd | 1.95 | 2.15 | 2.49 |
| `msg0` – `emit1` | 0.04 | 0.12 | 0.23 |

Crowding is the hub: a signal that reports it also reports resource and the coming stop, without the brain
having to encode either separately. `msg0` and `emit1` become partly redundant.

**Emitters only.** Restricted to agents that emit on the channel, `emit1` carries 0.25 bits about crowding and
0.10 about resource at the end, about as much as (crowding) or more than (resource) over all agents: how
strongly an agent emits is informative, not only whether it emits. At 1M the opposite holds for `emit2` (0.11 bits in the on/off
indicator, 0.02 among emitters). `emit0` among its emitters carried 0.16 bits about resource at 500k and none
from 750k on.

**Shared environment.** Conditioning on the nearest fertility patch rarely lowers the MI and often raises it
(for example `msg0`–crowding 0.34 overall, 0.35 within patches at the end). The associations are not an
artefact of many agents on the same rich patch.

**Lineage.** At 120k and 250k the within-clade MI is close to or above the population value (dialects of
co-existing lineages). At the end it is about a third of it for crowding (0.11 of 0.34 bits for `msg0`), and
almost nothing for `msg0`→thrust (0.02 of 0.29).

The per-state tables (MI, null mean and z for every channel, target and variant, plus the kin-given-crowding
table) are in [`signals/tables.md`](signals/tables.md), with trend tables per variant at the top.

similar ones.

## 4. Method

**Signals.** `Actions.emit` is not stored, so `signals.decode` re-runs S0–S8 of the step (weight flush,
neighbour search, sensing, brain, decode, solvency mask) as a pure function of the state, as `step.step` does.
A test checks that one step stores exactly the recomputed `msg` for every survivor and counts exactly the
recomputed emitters. Signals per alive agent:

- `emit0..2`: the deposit quanta `floor(256·emit_c)` of the three chemical channels (§13.3), which is what
  reaches the field. An agent with `emit_c < 1/256` deposits nothing; "emitter" means `q_c > 0`, as in the
  `emitters_frac` metric. Channel 0 is local and short-lived (decay 0.1), channel 1 intermediate (0.03),
  channel 2 long-lived and wide (decay 0.005, diffusion 0.24).
- `msg0`, `msg1`: the message components, in (−1, 1). Neighbours sense `msg0` individually (the three nearest
  neighbour blocks and the bond slots) and both components as the neighbourhood mean.

**Targets**, at the start of the step: `resource` (quanta in the agent's field cell), `harvest`
(`last_harvest`), `energy`, `crowd` (sensed neighbours, 0–8), `kin` and `nonkin` (same- and other-clade sensed
neighbours), `close_kin` (sensed parents, children and siblings). **Intent**: `thrust_next`, `turn_next` and
`attack_next`, decoded from the state one compiled step later, for the agents that survive it.

**Estimator.** `mi.mi_knn`: Kraskov–Stögbauer–Grassberger estimator 1, k = 10, on ranks with ties broken at
random. MI is invariant under monotone maps, and random tie-breaking preserves it exactly: the original value
is recoverable from the rank, and the order within a tie is independent noise. For an integer variable this
equals dithering by `U(−½, ½)`. It matters here because the signals and most targets are integers with large
atoms (half or more of the agents at `q = 0`, counts 0–8, a boolean). On such synthetic data the
discrete-continuous estimator of Gao et al. (2017) was biased by +0.07 bits; dithered KSG by less than 0.001.
On synthetic data the estimator gives about 0 for independent inputs, the closed form for correlated Gaussians,
and the entropy (within 5 %) for a deterministic discrete channel (`tests/test_analysis_mi.py`). A Miller–Madow
plug-in on 8 equal-frequency bins is computed as a cross-check (JSON only).

**Null and effect size.** 100 shuffles of the signal across agents, within strata for the conditional
variants; z = (MI − null mean) / null sd. With 225 tests per state, a cell counts as significant at
z ≥ 4 (one-sided normal p ≈ 3·10⁻⁵, about 0.01 after Bonferroni). A permutation p cannot resolve that with 100
shuffles, so it is in the JSON only. The effect size is the excess over the null mean, in bits. Samples: 6,000
random alive agents per state (of about 32,000) and, per channel, a separate sample of up to 6,000 emitters. The
null sd is about 0.0075 bits, so z = 4 is an excess of about 0.03 bits.

**Variants.**

- `all`: the agent sample.
- `emitters`: `I(q_c; Y | q_c > 0)`, emitters of that channel only.
- `emits`: `I(1[q_c > 0]; Y)`. Together with `all` and `emitters` it gives the chain rule
  `I(q; Y) = I(1[q > 0]; Y) + P(q > 0)·I(q; Y | q > 0)` (JSON only).
- `patch`: `I(S; Y | nearest fertility patch)`, null shuffled within patches. Removes associations that only
  reflect where agents are.
- `clade`: `I(S; Y | own clade)`, null shuffled within clades (clades with at least 50 sampled agents; coverage
  in the JSON). Removes heritable differences, such as clades that both signal more and move more.
- `crowd`, kin targets only: `I(S; kin | crowd)`, whether a signal tracks kin composition beyond local density.

**Reproducibility.** The 1.49M replicate, 14k steps before the end, gives MI values correlated at 0.98 with the
end state. All 71 cells with an excess of at least 0.05 bits are significant in both; every cell whose
significance differs between the two has an excess below 0.044 bits. Throughout, **robust** means z ≥ 4 and an
excess of at least 0.05 bits.

**Reproduce.**

## 5. Caveats

- **Population-level MI measures a shared code.** It is high only when agents in the same situation emit alike.
  Many private codes (each lineage mapping its state differently) give little population MI even when every
  agent's signal is a function of its state. The `patch` and `clade` variants partly separate the two.
- **Sender information is an upper bound on what receivers get.** Chemical deposits mix in the field (decay,
  diffusion, other emitters on the same cell), and `msg1` reaches neighbours only as a mean.
- **Information is not honesty or function.** Any deterministic function of the inputs carries some information
  about them, and the brain's state feeds both the signal and the next action. A positive MI says the signal
  could inform a receiver; whether receivers use it, or whether it is honest, deceptive or neutral in Mitri et
  al.'s sense, needs the receivers' responses and their fitness consequences.
- **Targets are correlated.** `crowd` is nearly the same variable as `kin` (same-clade neighbours), and later in
  the run it is linked to resource and to next-step thrust; see §2.
- **Snapshots.** One state per time point, 6,000 of about 32,000 agents. The replicate bounds the noise; slower
  fluctuations (seasons are off, patches drift) are not sampled.
- **Code segments.** The next-step targets come from one step of the current code. For the 120k and later states
  that is the run's own step. For step 0 it differs from the run's first step (`f09b32a`) only in the rounding
  of the regeneration field, which cannot matter for the founders' next actions at the precision reported.

## 6. Next steps

- **Receiver side.** Estimate `I(sensed msg0 of a neighbour; the receiver's next action)` and its transfer
  entropy conditioned on the receiver's own crowding. Better, ablate: fork 1.49M with the received signals
  shuffled across agents and compare behaviour and fitness. That needs a new sensing switch, off by default
  (`POST_V01_KEYS`), so it is an engine change, not analysis. Only the receiver side can classify the signals
  as used, honest or deceptive.
- **Cost.** Emission costs energy (`emit_cost` 20 per channel at full strength). The fall in emitters with a
  rise in information fits signals being kept where they pay; per-lineage emission rates against reproduction
  would test it.
- **Director arms.** Rerun this analysis on the §9 evaluation arms to see whether a changing environment keeps
  several codes alive (the 120k pattern) or collapses them into one (the end pattern).
